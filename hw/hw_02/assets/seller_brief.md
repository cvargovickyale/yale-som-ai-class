# Sanford & Hawley Seller Brief

## Seller identity

- **Company:** Sanford & Hawley
- **Website:** https://sanhaw.com/
- **Business type:** Building-materials supplier and lumber yard
- **Geographic footprint:** Connecticut and western Massachusetts, with public
  locations in Avon, Manchester, Unionville, and West Springfield, plus a
  Creative Design Gallery listing
- **History:** The company describes itself as the oldest business in
  Farmington, Connecticut, operating at the same location and under the same
  family since 1884. Its site says the business is now operated by Frank,
  Bob, Ted, and Bobby Sanford.
- **Positioning:** Quality building materials, a tradition of service, and
  practical support for contractor and homeowner customers.

## Why this business

The owner’s reason for choosing Sanford & Hawley is personal and local: an
interest in lumber and physical materials, the sensory experience of a lumber
yard, the company’s long history, and its family-owned character. This is
useful context for tone and motivation, but it is not a sales claim to make in
outreach unless the business owner chooses to use it.

## Products and services observed publicly

The website presents or links to:

- Lumber and building materials
- Commercial contractor accounts and commercial distribution
- Delivery
- A millwork shop
- Field service
- A drive-through lumber yard
- Decking and railing
- Moulding and related finish materials
- Account services
- A creative design gallery

The site explicitly distinguishes **Contractor** and **DIY/Homeowner**
audiences. It therefore supports a two-sided customer model: trade customers
who may buy repeatedly for projects, and consumers who buy directly for their
own work.

## Current customer knowledge and open questions

The public site does not establish the following facts. They should be
researched or confirmed before the agent treats them as facts:

- Which contractor specialties buy most often: home builders, remodelers,
  deck and fence contractors, kitchen/bath firms, finish carpenters,
  architects/design-build firms, commercial general contractors, or others
- Typical contractor revenue, employee count, annual material spend, project
  count, or account size
- Whether the strongest customers are independent local firms, regional firms,
  franchises, or a mixture
- The percentage of sales from contractors, commercial accounts, and
  DIY/homeowner customers
- Whether the company has a preferred customer size, minimum order size, or
  geographic delivery radius
- Which products and services have the strongest margins, repeat purchase
  rates, or capacity constraints
- Which customer roles make or influence buying decisions
- Whether there are existing customer segments or territories the company
  wants to avoid
- Public evidence of scandals or reputational issues; none should be inferred
  merely because a search did not find one

The agent must label these as unknowns until supported by a source or supplied
by Sanford & Hawley. In particular, it must not invent a DIY sales percentage,
contractor size distribution, named customer relationship, or account history.

## External-source review

The company website is useful for the seller’s own positioning, but it is not
enough to understand customer experience or reputation. These outside sources
should be used as triangulation, not as substitutes for company records.

### Yelp — customer-experience signal

- **Source:** [Yelp search for Sanford & Hawley in Connecticut](https://www.yelp.com/search?find_desc=Sanford%20%26%20Hawley&find_loc=Connecticut)
- Yelp can surface comments about wait times, product availability, staff
  helpfulness, pricing, delivery, and the in-person lumber-yard experience.
- Yelp reviews are self-selected and are especially likely to overrepresent
  customers with unusually good or unusually bad experiences—the “salty
  reviews” concern is real. Review count, recency, location, reviewer history,
  and repeated themes matter more than one dramatic review.
- During this pass Yelp’s public search was rate-limited, so no rating,
  review count, or review text is recorded here. The agent must not invent
  those values or treat an inaccessible listing as evidence of either good or
  bad service.
- For later research, capture: `review_date`, `location_reviewed`,
  `customer_type_if_stated`, `theme`, `sentiment`, `specificity`, and
  `evidence_url`.

### Better Business Bureau — reputation cross-check

- **Source:** [BBB search for Sanford Hawley in Connecticut](https://www.bbb.org/search?find_text=Sanford%20Hawley&find_loc=Connecticut)
- BBB is useful for checking whether there is a verified business profile,
  accreditation status, rating, and complaint history.
- The public search checked for this brief returned unrelated businesses rather
  than a verified Sanford & Hawley profile. That is a limitation of the search,
  not evidence that Sanford & Hawley has no complaints or a clean record.
- Therefore the brief makes no scandal, accreditation, rating, or complaint
  claim. If a direct BBB profile is found later, record the profile date and
  distinguish verified complaints from general reviews.

These external sources are most useful for identifying questions to ask and
risks to investigate. They should not be used to assert that a particular
contractor is a customer of Sanford & Hawley, nor should negative reviews be
copied into outreach.

## Initial target hypothesis

The most promising initial segments to test are independent or locally rooted
contractors within Sanford & Hawley’s practical service area whose work
regularly consumes lumber or related materials: custom and production
homebuilders, remodelers, deck/fence/outdoor-living contractors, and
commercial or design-build firms. This is a hypothesis for research, not a
claim that these segments currently account for a particular share of sales.
